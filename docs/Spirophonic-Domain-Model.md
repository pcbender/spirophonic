# Spirophonic Domain Model

Status: **conceptual exploration**
This document defines the primary ideas and relationships in Spirophonic. It is not yet an implementation plan or an approved technical contract.

## Core Idea

Spirophonic is an instrument for composing with interacting cyclic systems.

Shapes, rhythms, pitches, colors, and motion do not need to be translated from one another. They can arise as sibling expressions of the same underlying relationships.

The central generative act is not the drawing of a curve. It is an encounter between moving systems.

```text
Wheels + Fields + Time
          ↓
      Encounters
          ↓
  Perceptible expressions
    ├── Shape
    ├── Rhythm
    ├── Pitch
    ├── Color
    ├── Motion
    └── Control
```

A Wheel does not necessarily play itself.

A Wheel may play a Field, another Wheel, or the changing relationship between them.

## Foundational Principles

### Relationship first

The primary subject of Spirophonic is not geometry, sound, or notation. It is relationship becoming perceptible.

### Motion before trace

A Wheel is a cyclic generator moving through space. The visible trace is a record of that motion, not the Wheel itself.

### Encounters produce events

Musically meaningful events arise when Heads cross, touch, approach, align with, or otherwise encounter Fields, boundaries, traces, and other Heads.

### Closure is optional

A Wheel may generate a closed, drifting, damped, expanding, quasiperiodic, or open trajectory. Musical structure does not require the visible trace to close.

### Time is independent of geometry

A relationship may close once per bar, several times per bar, across many bars, or never exactly close. The Transport determines how the system is observed and organized musically.

### Determinism includes variation

Given the complete set of inputs, Spirophonic produces the same result. Variation and randomness are permitted only when represented as explicit, reproducible inputs.

## Primary Objects

## Composition

A **Composition** is the complete Spirophonic work.

It contains:

* Wheels and their Heads;
* Fields and their boundaries;
* Parts that interpret encounters;
* Instruments that perform musical events;
* a Transport that defines musical time;
* optional variation specifications;
* optional Recordings of generated performances.

A Composition describes a generative system. It does not need to contain a fixed sequence of notes.

## Space

**Space** is the shared coordinate system in which Wheels, Heads, Fields, traces, and encounters exist.

Space provides:

* a center or set of centers;
* position;
* distance;
* angle;
* orientation;
* scale;
* spatial relationships between objects.

Several Wheels may be concentric, offset from one another, or moving around different centers.

Space is not inherently musical. Parts decide how spatial facts are interpreted.

## Wheel

A **Wheel** is a cyclic or continuously evolving generator that carries one or more Heads through Space.

A Wheel may be based on:

* a spirogram or rolling-circle mechanism;
* a Lissajous relationship;
* a rose relationship;
* a harmonograph;
* an ellipse;
* a superformula;
* another periodic or quasiperiodic motion system.

A Wheel has properties such as:

* center;
* orientation;
* phase;
* rate;
* direction;
* amplitude or scale;
* internal ratios;
* damping;
* modulation;
* initial conditions.

The word *Wheel* describes the generating motion, not the shape of the completed trace.

A Wheel can be cyclic even when its Head does not return to the same spatial position.

```text
Wheel phase may repeat
Head position may drift
Trace may remain open
Encounters may continue to develop
```

## Head

A **Head** is a tracked point carried or generated by a Wheel.

It may be understood as a pen tip, playhead, sensor, or point of attention.

A Wheel may carry:

* one Head;
* several Heads at different offsets;
* several Heads at different phases;
* Heads with different encounter rules.

At a given time, a Head has a state such as:

```ts
type HeadState = {
  time: number
  position: {
    x: number
    y: number
  }
  velocity: {
    x: number
    y: number
  }
  speed: number
  angle: number
  radius: number
  wheelPhase: number
}
```

The Head’s state should be calculated from the Composition and time, not from the display frame rate.

## Trace

A **Trace** is the path left by a Head over a period of observation.

A Trace may be:

* closed;
* open;
* damped;
* expanding;
* drifting;
* retraced;
* temporary;
* retained as part of a performance.

A Trace is one projection of Wheel motion.

It may also become an encounter target. One Head can cross the Trace produced by another Head.

The Trace must not be confused with the Wheel that generated it.

## Field

A **Field** is a system of spatial references or boundaries that Heads can encounter.

A Field may be stationary or may move independently.

Examples include:

* concentric circles;
* concentric ellipses;
* radial spokes;
* rotating spokes;
* spirals;
* bands;
* grids;
* another Wheel’s active or accumulated Trace.

A Field supplies structure without dictating a musical meaning.

The same Field can be interpreted differently by different Parts.

## Boundary

A **Boundary** is an individual element within a Field.

Examples include:

* one circle in a concentric circular Field;
* one ellipse in an elliptical Field;
* one spoke in a metrical Field;
* the inner or outer edge of a radial band;
* one retained Trace.

A Boundary has a stable identity so that an encounter can refer to it directly.

```ts
type Boundary = {
  id: string
  fieldId: string
  index: number
  kind: 'circle' | 'ellipse' | 'spoke' | 'band' | 'trace'
}
```

The Boundary index may later be interpreted as a pitch, register, rhythmic subdivision, instrument, chord member, or control value.

## Ring and Ellipse Fields

Concentric circles provide radial structure.

A Head crossing outward or inward through a sequence of circles creates an ordered stream of encounters.

```text
inner ring → lower region
outer ring → higher region
```

That ordering may become pitch, register, intensity, orchestration, or another musical dimension.

Concentric ellipses add orientation to the radial structure. A Head’s relationship to an ellipse depends on both its distance and its angle.

Rotating or modulating ellipses can create gradual change even when the Wheel itself remains stable.

Periodic Fields may use structures such as:

```text
5
8
12
21
```

These numbers are properties of the Field, not fixed musical meanings.

A five-boundary Field might be interpreted as five scale degrees by one Part and five rhythmic regions by another.

Several periodic Fields may coexist. Their local recurrences can create larger-scale form through their changing alignments.

## Spoke

A **Spoke** is a center-anchored reference vector, commonly used to project musical meter into Space.

For example:

```text
4 spokes  → quarter-note reference
8 spokes  → eighth-note reference
12 spokes → triplet subdivisions in 4/4
16 spokes → sixteenth-note reference
```

A Head crossing a Spoke gains a metrical address.

The crossing does not need to occur exactly on a quantized musical grid. The Spoke tells us where the encounter lies relative to that grid.

Spokes may be:

* fixed to the Composition;
* rotated by the Transport;
* attached to a Wheel;
* independently rotating;
* grouped into bars, beats, and subdivisions.

This allows Spirophonic to represent:

* global meter;
* local Wheel phase;
* polymeter;
* phase displacement;
* nested rhythmic cycles.

## Vector

A **Vector** is a measured directional relationship between two points or objects.

Unlike a Spoke, a Vector does not need to belong to a Field.

For example, the Vector from Head A to Head B has:

* length;
* direction;
* rotational rate;
* rate of expansion or contraction.

These properties may express musical tension, convergence, divergence, panning, filtering, or other continuous control.

```text
distance between Heads       → tension or register
angle between Heads          → pan or harmonic orientation
rate of approach             → crescendo or transition
rotation of connecting Vector → modulation
```

## Encounter

An **Encounter** is a meaningful relationship detected between objects in Space.

Encounters are the central event source in Spirophonic.

Examples include:

* Head crossing Ring;
* Head crossing Ellipse;
* Head crossing Spoke;
* Head crossing another Head’s Trace;
* two Heads occupying approximately the same position;
* two Heads reaching minimum separation;
* Heads sharing a radius;
* Heads aligning by angle;
* Heads moving in the same or opposite direction.

An Encounter is a physical or relational fact. It is not yet a note.

## Crossing

A **Crossing** is an Encounter in which a Head passes through a Boundary.

A Crossing may include:

```ts
type Crossing = {
  id: string
  timeSeconds: number

  headId: string
  boundaryId: string

  position: {
    x: number
    y: number
  }

  direction:
    | 'inward'
    | 'outward'
    | 'clockwise'
    | 'counterclockwise'

  speed: number
  incidenceAngle: number
  wheelPhase: number

  bar?: number
  barPhase?: number
}
```

Crossing properties can support musical interpretations such as:

```text
crossing time        → onset
boundary index       → pitch or scale degree
direction            → articulation or melodic direction
speed                → velocity
position angle       → pan
incidence angle      → timbre
time inside a band   → note duration
```

Entering a band may begin a note. Leaving it may end the note. Duration can therefore emerge from an encounter rather than being assigned afterward.

## Relation Event

A **Relation Event** is a broader Encounter that may not involve a fixed Boundary.

```ts
type RelationEventKind =
  | 'boundary-crossing'
  | 'trace-crossing'
  | 'conjunction'
  | 'closest-approach'
  | 'radial-alignment'
  | 'angular-alignment'
  | 'opposition'
  | 'direction-match'
```

For two concentric Wheels, such as a harmonograph and a spirogram, Relation Events may include:

```text
Harmonograph Head crosses Spirogram Trace
Spirogram Head crosses Harmonograph Trace
Heads occupy the same region simultaneously
Heads share a radius
Heads align across the center
Heads reach their closest approach
```

These encounters can create musical structure without requiring either trajectory to close.

## Part

A **Part** is a musical interpretation of selected Encounters.

A Part determines:

* which Wheels, Heads, Fields, or Boundaries it observes;
* which Encounter kinds it accepts;
* how encounters become onsets;
* how pitch is selected;
* how velocity and duration are formed;
* whether events are quantized;
* whether continuous relationships become control signals;
* which Instrument performs the result.

A Part does not own a Wheel or Field.

Several Parts may interpret the same Encounter differently.

For example, a single 3:2 relationship might support:

```text
Part A: x-related encounters → three percussion events
Part B: y-related encounters → two percussion events
Part C: ratio 3:2 → root and perfect fifth
Part D: relative phase → filter modulation
```

The Encounter is shared. The interpretations are separate.

## Musical Event

A **Musical Event** is the result of a Part interpreting an Encounter.

```ts
type MusicalEvent = {
  id: string
  sourceEncounterId: string
  partId: string
  instrumentId: string

  timeSeconds: number
  bar?: number
  beat?: number

  kind: 'note' | 'control' | 'rest'

  note?: number
  frequencyHz?: number
  velocity?: number
  durationSeconds?: number
  controlValue?: number
}
```

A Musical Event represents musical intention. Later performance variation may alter its exact timing, velocity, duration, or articulation.

## Instrument

An **Instrument** determines how a Part’s Musical Events are rendered as sound.

It may describe:

* a percussion voice;
* a synthesizer;
* a browser preview sound;
* a MIDI channel and program;
* a soundfont;
* an external instrument;
* a future OSC or SuperCollider destination.

The Instrument performs musical structure. It does not create the underlying relationships.

## Transport

The **Transport** places the Composition into musical and observational time.

It defines:

* tempo;
* meter;
* start and end;
* playback position;
* loop boundaries;
* performance duration;
* synchronization;
* the relationship between seconds, bars, beats, and Wheel phases.

The Transport also determines the observation window for systems that do not close.

```ts
type PerformanceWindow = {
  startSeconds: number
  durationSeconds: number
}
```

The former rule:

```text
One closed curve is one bar.
```

becomes an optional mapping rather than a foundational invariant.

The stronger principle is:

```text
A performance observes interacting Wheels over time.
Their encounters produce events.
```

## Recorder

The **Recorder** captures the events produced during a performance.

It should preserve several layers rather than flattening everything immediately into notes.

```text
Wheels and Fields
       ↓
Encounter Events
       ↓
Interpreted Musical Events
       ↓
Performed Musical Events
```

This separation preserves:

1. what happened relationally;
2. what the Part intended musically;
3. what was finally performed after variation.

## Recording

A **Recording** is a durable event-based artifact created by the Recorder.

It may contain:

```ts
type SpirophonicRecording = {
  version: string
  engineVersion: string
  name: string

  sourceSnapshot: Composition

  performanceWindow: PerformanceWindow
  seed?: string
  variation?: VariationSpec

  encounters: EncounterEvent[]
  interpretedEvents: MusicalEvent[]
  performedEvents: MusicalEvent[]
}
```

A Recording supports two distinct operations.

### Replay

Play the captured performed events exactly.

This does not require recalculating Wheel motion or Encounters.

### Reinterpretation

Pass the recorded Encounter Events through different Parts.

A performance originally interpreted as percussion may later become melody, harmony, automation, or visual control.

```text
Encounter Recording
        ↓
New Parts
        ↓
New Musical Performance
```

The relationship performance can therefore be recorded once and interpreted many ways.

## Export

Exporters consume canonical recorded or generated events.

```text
Musical Events
  ├── Browser preview
  ├── MIDI
  ├── Strudel
  ├── Tidal
  ├── OSC
  └── future formats
```

MIDI primarily represents discrete musical information:

* onset;
* pitch;
* velocity;
* duration;
* channel;
* control changes.

Tidal and Strudel can also express broader cyclic and continuous relationships:

* pattern density;
* cycle position;
* probability;
* pan;
* cutoff;
* gain;
* sample choice;
* modulation.

The export format should not reach backward into Wheel geometry. It should consume recorded or resolved event data.

## Determinism

Spirophonic is deterministic when all inputs are specified.

```text
Composition
+ performance window
+ temporal resolution
+ encounter rules
+ variation settings
+ random seed
+ engine version
= reproducible Recording
```

The core must not depend on:

* the current clock time;
* display frame rate;
* browser speed;
* audio scheduling jitter;
* hidden calls to an unseeded random generator;
* unstable collection ordering.

Each Wheel should provide a stable state for a requested time:

```ts
wheel.stateAt(time)
```

Animation displays the calculated state. Animation timing does not determine the state.

## Variation

Variation is an explicit deterministic process.

```ts
type VariationSpec = {
  enabled: boolean
  seed: string

  wheel?: {
    phase?: number
    rate?: number
    amplitude?: number
    offset?: number
  }

  field?: {
    rotation?: number
    spacing?: number
    eccentricity?: number
  }

  interpretation?: {
    timing?: number
    velocity?: number
    probability?: number
    duration?: number
    pitchChoice?: number
  }
}
```

Variation may occur at several layers.

### Initial-condition variation

Changes the starting phase, orientation, offset, or rate of a Wheel or Field.

### Continuous variation

Applies slow seeded modulation to phase, damping, amplitude, rotation, spacing, or eccentricity.

### Interpretation variation

An Encounter always occurs, but a Part may respond only with a specified probability.

### Performance variation

Applies bounded timing, velocity, duration, articulation, or pitch changes after the Musical Event has been interpreted.

Every variation result should be derived from stable identities:

```text
seed
+ object ID
+ bar or cycle
+ event ID
= reproducible variation
```

Changing an unrelated Part should not unpredictably alter every later random decision.

## Example: Concentric Harmonograph and Spirogram

Consider a harmonograph Wheel and a spirogram Wheel sharing a center.

Each carries one Head.

The spirogram may provide relatively stable cyclic repetition. The harmonograph may gradually contract through damping.

Their Traces do not need to close.

During a defined performance window, Spirophonic can detect:

```text
Spirogram Head crossing Harmonograph Trace
Harmonograph Head crossing Spirogram Trace
Head conjunctions
shared-radius events
angular alignments
closest approaches
spoke crossings
ring or ellipse crossings
```

This naturally creates several time scales:

```text
Spirogram recurrence      → pulse or ostinato
Harmonograph development  → phrase or contour
Field encounters          → pitch and metrical structure
Mutual Wheel encounters   → accents, harmony, transitions
Transport                 → bars and larger form
```

Recognizable repetition and gradual change can coexist without forcing either Wheel into an artificial closed loop.

## Important Distinctions

### Wheel is not Trace

The Wheel is the generator. The Trace is the path left by its Head.

### Wheel is not necessarily closed

Cyclic generation does not require spatial closure.

### Field is not musical meaning

A Field supplies structure. Parts determine how that structure is interpreted.

### Encounter is not a note

An Encounter records what happened relationally. A Part decides what it becomes musically.

### Part is not Instrument

A Part defines musical interpretation. An Instrument performs the result.

### Closure is not a bar

Closure describes recurrence in a relationship. The Transport defines musical meter and form.

### Variation is not nondeterminism

A seeded variation process is part of the Composition’s input and remains reproducible.

### Recording is not merely export

A Recording preserves a performance as a reusable Spirophonic artifact. It can be replayed exactly or reinterpreted through different Parts.

## Defining Statement

```text
Spirophonic composes by placing moving Wheels in shared Space.

Their Heads encounter Fields, boundaries, traces, and one another.

Those encounters are recorded and interpreted as shape, rhythm, pitch,
color, motion, and sound.
```

The essence is not the Wheel.

The essence is not the Field.

The essence is the changing relationship made perceptible through their encounters.
